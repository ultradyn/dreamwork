The rule — three steps, and it decides everything

qaBranch(q) → [lead, root, branch]. lead is discussion that led to the root and keeps today's treatment, collapse and all. branch is one flat list at one depth. Never a tree.

1 · a resolution

Root is the answer. lead = follows[0:answer_at], branch = follows[answer_at:]. Needs no new signal — answer_at is the positional cut #128 already records.

2 · an explicit reply

Root is the contribution just above the first Reply (loop, …); if that reply is first, there is nothing to reply to and it stays flat. This step needs a tag that does not exist yet.

3 · otherwise

Flat. Your tie-breaker, and it generalises: prefer flat over wrongly-attached. A flat render under-claims; a wrong branch makes a false claim about who answered whom.

Never derive attachment from timestamps. File order is the chronology. A stamp comparison is a second mechanism able to disagree with the first — and it would disagree exactly on the entries whose stamps are missing or hand-edited. Stamps render; they never decide structure.

What it looks like, and what it announces
No new component. .thread's 1px --line rail and 1ch inset are the one depth. A second container would be a second answer to a question the page already answered.
The rail starts at the root's bottom edge, so the connection is drawn rather than implied. Rows keep you/loop, the stamp, and the luminance split. No accent — that is for live, actionable things.
The branch never collapses. It is where a note you just wrote lands. Only the segment a resolution has already answered may fold.
Narrow: keep the rail, drop the padding. The cost is ~1ch once, not per turn — which is the whole reason N1 caps the depth.
Nesting is announced, not merely drawn. The exchange becomes one <ul>; the branch is a nested <ul> inside the root's own <li>. A sibling <ul> looks identical and reads flat — which is exactly the check that catches it. The composer stays outside the branch: one input per card, and a box inside a branch promises "reply to this turn", which /comment cannot keep.
Motion — nothing new is authored

Every case already has a cell in transitions.md's state matrix, so the design names the existing gesture rather than inventing a second one.

How it would be proven

Five frozen fixtures — rooted, no root, two answers, root with no branch, unrecognised tag — and eight checks, each naming the production line that reddens it.

Two of these would have been hollow, and the spec says so. "No branch" is satisfied by "no contributions" unless the fixture's contribution count is asserted at runtime. And the two-answer check cannot distinguish "rooted at the last answer" from "the first answer never existed", because the parser discards it first — so the check records what it does not prove instead of being believed as the thing it proxies. A motion check asserts a part-way frame over a ≤1400ms window and that the node was never replaced, so a later tick cannot supply the result. At least one guard drives the dashboard, not just /questions.
Decisions wanted — one line each
D1
Is a root a resolution only — never merely the first loop contribution?
Rec: yes, as written. Promoting a Follow-up is inference from adjacency, which is what N2 was rejected for, and it inverts on the common shape where the loop asks a clarifying question and you answer it: the loop's question becomes the root and your answer a reply to it. Cost of yes: your screenshot's card stays flat until follow-up 1 lands. Overrule this one line and it is fixed at the price of that inversion.
D2
At most one branch per question?
Rec: yes. It is what keeps the rule a procedure rather than a tree walk, and the only reading of "a single inset depth" that survives a long exchange.
D3
## Answered: flat, or threaded?
Rec: flat. There the resolution head sits at the start of the body — positionally before every sub-bullet, while temporally it may follow several. Rooting at it attaches every pre-fold note as a reply to the resolution, which is a wrong attachment; the alternative is a timestamp cut, which the rule forbids.
D4–D7
The smaller four.
D4 a resolution outranks an explicit reply tag, so the two mechanisms can never disagree. D5 real <ul>/<li> nesting, not ARIA on divs. D6 narrow keeps the rail and drops the padding. D7 an empty branch emits no container. Rec: all four as written.
Approve the rule and D1–D7, or name the one line to change. Follow-up 1 is the one I would ask you to green-light alongside it.
