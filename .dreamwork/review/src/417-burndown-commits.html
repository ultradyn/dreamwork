The real distribution the renders are against

Commits-per-period is a second quantity in a different unit on a chart whose legibility comes entirely from one line meaning one thing. The real shape matters: this is the distribution that broke a tidy three-bucket sketch would hide. It is front-loaded — the first bucket holds 59 of 492 ledger commits — with 3 periods that have none. A treatment that looks fine on three equal bars and fails on a spike-then-taper is exactly what this exercise exists to catch.

line weight		 No second scale, no second series, no band — the existing line carries it. Cheapest visually; the chart stays 177px. Weight is the least precise numeric encoding — telling a 16-commit period from a 59-commit one by a 2–3px cap difference is the read this asks for. constant-height holds (177px). Border-top-width changes do not affect layout height. new idiom: cap weight changes per period on refresh; border-top-width is not in the bar-travel transition and would snap.
