# A helper is only as general as its callers have forced it to be

dreamer-qsec, 2026-07-25 17:45. Task: #196, `6d58182`.

## What landed

The dashboard's questions fold (`.qsec`) travels instead of snapping. It reuses
the card fold rather than authoring a second gesture — `travelCard` for the
height, `revealBody` for the arrival, `dreamAway` for the departure — and *not*
`regroupCards`, because the cards inside have no geometry while the section is
shut and a FLIP from a zero rect slides in from the page's corner. Guard:
`dev/capture/qsec.mjs`, wired into `just guards`.

## The thing worth carrying: two shared helpers were right by coincidence

The fix touched three lines of new behaviour and two lines of latent bug, and
the two bugs are the same bug:

- **`travelCard` interpolated a border-box measurement into a content-box
  property.** `getBoundingClientRect().height` includes padding; CSS `height`
  does not. That had never mattered, because its only callers were `.qa` and
  `.git .commit` and neither has vertical padding. The first caller that did —
  a `<details>`, carrying #169's `.5rem` of air — played the travel 16px past
  where it ends and snapped back when the inline height was cleared.
- **`dreamAway` stripped the ghost's identity from the node and not its
  subtree.** "A corpse holds no address" was complete while every ghost was one
  card or one row, where the node *was* the whole identity. The section ghost
  is a clone of an entire open `<details>` carrying `data-keep="qsec"` and
  three `data-qid` cards; `snapshotFolds` takes the last `details[data-keep]`
  and a ghost is appended last, so the section re-opened under him one tick
  after he shut it.

Neither helper was wrong when written. Both had invariants that held only
because of the shape of their callers, and neither said so. **When a shared
helper acquires its first caller of a genuinely new shape, re-derive its
invariants rather than reading its comment** — the comment was written by
someone who could not distinguish "true" from "true of everything that calls
this".

## A justification you can check is the most dangerous kind when it is false

The section snapped for a reason that was written down twice — in `watch.py`
and in `watch-design.md` — and stated confidently: this is a standalone
`expand` "because nothing that MOVES sits below the toggle". Reviews, files,
status and the tint picker are all below the toggle. It is a one-line thing to
check and nobody checked it for the life of #141, because a *reasoned*
exemption reads as settled in a way that a bare "TODO: animate this" never
would. #169 then visited `.qsec` deliberately, asserted its padding and its
colour, and did not notice the section was not animating at all — the guard
looked at the surface the paragraph had already excused.

Both texts now carry the correction rather than a quiet edit, because the
false-and-checkable shape is the reusable part, not the fix.

## And the remainder is stated as unexamined, not as decided

The plain `expand()` peeks — dreams, the archive, the `.md` files, the status
overflow — still toggle instantly, and the test that used to excuse them is
exactly the one that just proved false about `.qsec`: every one of them has
panels below it. That is now written in `transitions.md` as *unexamined rather
than decided*, which is the honest state and the one that does not re-arm the
same trap. Worth a task.

## Guard notes

- The trace is bounded to **1500ms**, and the assertion is the count of
  distinct positions the panel *below* the section visits: 2 before, ~47 after.
- The second assertion is that **no frame goes past the final position**. That
  is the border-box failure stated as the thing it does, and unlike a
  "has it arrived by t=950ms" check it does not depend on when the traced
  pointer click actually landed — at 1246px of travel, a fixed offset measured
  a perfectly clean ease as 32px short and the guard was reporting its own
  latency.
- The ghost-address check is driven over a **real tick** (`POST /command` to
  move the mtime, then `tick()`), because the damage is a re-render landing
  inside the ghost's ~1s life and the tick polls on its own 2s phase — sleeping
  and hoping catches it half the time, and a guard that is right half the time
  trains you to rerun it. It reports whether the ghost was present, so it
  states its own vacuity.
- Shown red three ways: the bug itself, `box-sizing` deleted (16.0px overshot,
  exactly `2 × .5rem`), and the subtree strip reverted (section re-opens).
