# dreamhub stage 1 — planning it, and the thing I nearly recommended

Task #96, plan only. Deliverable landed as
`.dreamwork/docs/plans/dreamhub-stage1.md` (403ab25).

## What I nearly got wrong

I started out intending to write the plan `daemon-mode.md` already
implied: `/` lists projects, `/{project}/…` reverse-proxies to that
project's watch port. When I read `watch.py` to size it, the fetches were
root-absolute, and my first instinct was the clever fix — inject a tiny
shim into the proxied HTML that monkey-patches `window.fetch` and
`history.pushState` to prepend the prefix. Thirty lines, generic over
watch.py's internals, no coupling to its source strings. I liked it.

It is wrong, and the way it is wrong is the interesting part. A shim can
patch what the page **calls** — `fetch`, `pushState`, `replaceState` are
all interceptable globals. It cannot patch what the page **reads**:
`isInternal()` and `routeOf()` compare `a.pathname` and
`location.pathname` against the literals `'/'`, `'/questions'`, `'/file'`,
`'/review'`. Those are local functions in a generated string. Nothing
from outside reaches them.

So the shim would have shipped something worse than a broken feature: a
prefixed deep link would have *loaded*, *rendered*, and shown the
dashboard instead of the view you asked for. No error, no 404, no clue.
The two thirds of the problem the shim fixes are the two thirds that fail
loudly; the third it can't touch is the one that fails silently. That
asymmetry is not a coincidence — an intercepted call goes wrong at the
network boundary where something notices, and a mis-read route goes wrong
in a `===` where nothing does.

The lesson generalises past proxies: when deciding whether a wrapper can
adapt something, split its surface into what it calls and what it reads.
The reads are where wrappers lose.

## Two things I decided rather than asked

Being sparing with his turns meant answering things myself that I could
have parked:

- **Origin-per-project instead of the prefix.** His sketch said
  `/{project}/…`. I deviated, with the reasoning in the plan and one
  non-blocking question at the end. What made me comfortable: `ssh -L`
  gives a local port per remote project, so origin-per-project is not a
  local-only shortcut that collapses at stage 3 — it survives the swarm.
  If that weren't true I would have had to ask, because it would have
  been a whether, not a how.
- **`/data.json` as the shared contract instead of shared code.** The
  question "does the hub parse `questions.md` itself?" answers itself
  once you notice watch.py already computes `open_questions` and serves
  it. The hub polls `/mtime` (which is also the liveness check) and
  re-reads `/data.json` on change — the same protocol watch's own client
  uses. That is reuse at the layer that doesn't create an owner.

## Where the ownership rule bit, and where it didn't

The disjointness invariant held up well: stage 1 creates four new paths
and touches two shared docs. But two of the nine increments wanted
something owned — the guard list in the justfile, and (under the proxy
design) watch.py itself. The justfile one resolves to a handoff line; the
watch.py one is why the design changed. Worth noticing that **ownership
pressure produced a better architecture**, not just a slower one: being
unable to edit watch.py forced the protocol-level reuse, which is the
design I'd want even with the file free.

The thing I'd watch for in the build: `dev/hub/` and `dev/capture/` are
sibling directories with the same `(OUT, PORT)` guard contract and no
shared runner. That is fine while they have different owners and wrong
the moment they don't.

## Out of scope, captured for the coordinator

1. Teach `watch.py` a URL prefix — three sites (fetches,
   `navigate`/pushState, `routeOf`/`isInternal`) — inside #124's
   server-core seam.
2. Wire the hub guards into `just test` (one line, justfile owner).
3. A light `/summary.json` on watch so an aggregator doesn't pull full
   document text over an ssh link — stage 3, not now, but it should not
   get invented twice.

Also: `/data.json` currently returns the full text of DREAMWORK.md,
questions.md and lessons.md. Fine locally on a change-triggered fetch,
which is why stage 1 doesn't care; it is exactly the thing that stops
being fine over a link.
