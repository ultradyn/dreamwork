Two things your ruling moved, and I have taken both

Every number the rail design rests on was measured against a 96px one-line flag. A worst-case ~6-word two-line tab is 180 × 32.3px. That is not a rounding error, and it falsifies the cliff the design was built on. Both consequences below follow from your ruling rather than adding to it, so I have taken them and they are reversible if you disagree.

830px, the real cliff. The tab fits inside the wrap down to 830 by half a pixel, is past the wrap at 820, and is clipped past the page edge at 810 and at 780
180px wide at worst. Typical authored labels are 117–130px — which is exactly why the worst case is what decides a no-truncation design
29.2px between the closest adjacent blocks in the artifact that needs marks most, against a 32.3px tab — so two tabs can collide
0artifacts changed so far. Increment 1 is parser-only: no tab, no CSS, no rebuild

The clipping is not a projection. This is the prototype tab at 780px, the width the design called the last one that works — and the table underneath it, in the same screenshot, is the design doc asserting that it does:

So the switch moves to the measured wrap-fit boundary rather than the literal 780, because a flag clipped mid-word is worse than no flag. And two marks closer than a tab height are the renderer’s problem — it offsets them — not an authoring rule you have to remember.

Why the strip is a real question and not a detail

Above the cliff the flags live in space the page was already wasting: the reading column is a fixed 613.5px and left-aligned, leaving 506px of empty wrap at 1280px. The rail costs you nothing you were using. Below the cliff that space does not exist — the margin outside the wrap is 16px at every width from 1120px down — so a strip has to take room from the text.

3rows a 7-mark strip needs at worst (~214px). Typical labels: 2 rows, ~140px
1row is what 3 marks fit in. So the strip works today and breaks at your own soft cap
16px outside the wrap at every width from 1120px down — the page has no outer margin to lend, at any narrow size
613.5px reading column, fixed and left-aligned. Above the cliff the rail is free; below it, every px comes off the text

This is the second time this feature has run into the same wall from a different direction. The first measurement ruled out a per-section list because the artifact needing flags most has 22 sections and a 22-entry list is a table of contents, not a set of postits. A 3-row strip is that same object again: too much furniture to skim, arriving in the place with the least room for it.

One decision: what do 5–7 marks become below the cliff?
A · Pay the chrome. The strip grows to as many rows as the marks need — up to 3 rows and ~214px above the text.
Complete, honest, truncates nothing. But it spends 214px of a narrow screen on an index you would act on by pressing next anyway, and it puts the most furniture exactly where there is least room.
B · Bounded strip, then “+3 more”. Show what fits in one row; next/prev still walks all of them.
Rec: reject. This is the worst of both — it keeps the chrome and becomes an incomplete index, so the flags you can see are not all the flags. And it is a truncation of the set rather than of a label, which is the argument you already rejected, one level up.
C · Just the walk. No labels in the chrome below the cliff: one row with next/prev and “3 of 7”, and the label appears at the mark you land on.
Rec: take it. ~32px instead of 214. Nothing is truncated — every label appears in full, at the moment it is relevant. Every mark stays reachable. And it is what you asked for in the same breath as the flags: “then i could have a next/prev button too”.

The honest cost, which is yours to price: C loses the at-a-glance overview. If you read reviews on a narrow window and want to know what the essentials are before walking them, C makes you walk to find out. A says that overview is worth 214px; C says it is not. I think it is not, because on a narrow screen you are reading linearly anyway — but you are the one who reads these.
What answering this unblocks, and what it does not
Answering this releases increment 2b, the below-cliff behaviour. It is not needed for increment 2a — the rail, the tab and next/prev above the cliff — which is fully specified by your 05:35 ruling plus the measurement, and whose brief is written and ready. 2a renders nothing below the cliff rather than a provisional strip, deliberately: a provisional strip is the thing that ships and then gets argued with when you rule.

Answering does not commit you to the two consequences I took above — the 830px boundary and renderer-side offsetting. Both are reversible and both are called out so you can overrule either without re-opening the design.
