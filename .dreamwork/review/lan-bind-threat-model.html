Who is trusted on the LAN?

decision review · task #233 · no non-local bind until answered

The request is technically simple; the authority change is not. Today binding to 127.0.0.1 is part of the dashboard's authorisation model. Listening on the LAN lets other devices read project files and call answer, ask, command, comment and tint writes.

Host and Origin checks are necessary, but they are not login. They stop DNS rebinding and a foreign website driving your browser. They do not stop another program or person already on the same network.
Choose the threat model
A · trusted LAN · recommended narrow scope

No login. You explicitly name the bind address, accepted Host names/IPs and advertised URL. Any client on the trusted LAN that can reach the socket and use an allowed Host is authorised to read and write.

Appropriate for a controlled home/dev LAN. Explicitly unsupported for public Wi-Fi, WAN forwarding or hostile peers.

B · authenticated network access

Do not bind non-locally yet. First design identity, session/token lifecycle, TLS or trusted reverse-proxy boundaries, logout/revocation and secret handling.

Safer for untrusted networks, but materially larger than the requested bind/hostname feature.

What option A would ship
default
127.0.0.1, unchanged.
listen
Singular --bind ADDRESS; wildcard is explicit.
authority
Repeatable exact --allow-host HOST; no wildcards, suffix matching, interface discovery or reverse DNS.
browser URL
Explicit --url-host HOST for wildcard binds, contained in the allowlist. Never print/open 0.0.0.0 or ::.
request gate
Validate exactly one Host before every route; validate present browser Origin before every POST and before reading/witnessing its body.
IPv6
Bracketed literals, explicit AF_INET6 server, no promise that :: also accepts IPv4.
warning
Startup prints trusted LAN · unauthenticated and the exact exposure boundary.

Example after approval:

watch.py --target . --bind 0.0.0.0 --allow-host xsm --allow-host 192.168.1.20 --url-host xsm
